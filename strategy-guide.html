THE LAST SIGNAL // FIELD MANUAL

Strategy Guide

A deeper progression guide covering efficient build orders, scarce resources, boss preparation, the Carrier Key, and recovery from stalled runs.

Original game guide · Updated July 2026

Core strategic principles

The Last Signal rewards balanced growth more than extreme specialization. Signal determines how quickly advanced purchases become affordable, but signal alone cannot buy equipment without scrap. Power determines how quickly you can gather scrap, yet a large battery without regeneration eventually empties. Combat stats open richer locations, while range and story progression decide whether those locations are reachable. The strongest plans improve one bottleneck before moving to the next.

Because the campaign is finite, efficiency is not about maximizing a number forever. It is about reaching each new tier with enough resources left to act. Spending every ration on travel or every point of intel on optional upgrades can slow the final route even when the purchases are individually useful.

Recommended opening sequence

  1. Listen until the first transmissions establish the story and begin revealing coordinates.
  2. Scavenge enough scrap for the Wire Antenna and Battery Bank.
  3. Buy the Wire Antenna first when signal is the limiting resource; buy the Battery Bank first when you are frequently waiting on power.
  4. Build the Sharpened Bar before committing to repeated combat.
  5. Work toward Solar Cloth. Automatic power regeneration transforms the game from isolated action bursts into a sustainable loop.
  6. Add the Salvaged Mast to increase both signal generation and range.

This order is not mandatory. A lucky sequence of caches or combat rewards can justify changing it. The important idea is that one economy upgrade, one power upgrade, and one damage upgrade should arrive before expensive specialization.

Managing signal, scrap, and power

Signal is time; scrap is activity

Signal accumulates even while you read the archive or leave the tab open. Scrap generally requires active scavenging or combat. When an upgrade needs both, start gathering scrap before the signal total is complete. Reaching the signal price and then discovering a large scrap deficit creates avoidable downtime.

Power regeneration changes priorities

Before Solar Cloth, a point of power is a limited action token. After Solar Cloth, power becomes renewable and maximum capacity becomes more valuable. Storm Capacitors are expensive, but the larger buffer is useful for long exploration sessions and distant travel.

Rations are a mobility reserve

Do not treat rations as ordinary healing potions. They are also required to enter every non-shelter location. Keep a small reserve for the next unlocked area. The Ration Rack becomes valuable earlier than its modest description suggests because each bonus ration supports either one trip or one rest.

Avoiding the intel dead end

Intel is spent by the Phase Array, Arc Cutter, Pulse Rifle, Storm Shell, Map Table, and Carrier Key. Buying every earlier intel upgrade can leave fewer than the six points required by the Carrier Key. The game now prevents this from becoming permanent through archive decoding, but planning still reduces the amount of signal you must convert later.

The Cipher Wheel should be purchased before collecting as many story transmissions as possible because it adds one intel to transmission rewards that already contain intel. It does not retroactively modify rewards collected before installation. Even so, the completed archive can always be decoded at Node 7 for one intel at the cost of 75 signal and 2 power.

Completionist route: Finish the seven transmissions, install the Cipher Wheel, return to Node 7, and use Decode archive until the Carrier Key’s intel requirement is met.

Useful build priorities

Fast economy

Wire Antenna → Salvaged Mast → Phase Array creates the strongest signal engine. Pair it with Battery Bank → Solar Cloth so the player can keep listening and scavenging while signal accumulates. This route reaches high prices quickly but must still purchase enough damage to survive key bosses.

Safe combat

Sharpened Bar → Arc Cutter plus Patch Vest → Ceramic Rig creates a reliable midgame. Guarding becomes more valuable as armor improves because reduced incoming damage stretches each health point. Storm Shell is powerful but costs intel that may be needed for the Carrier Key, so it is a luxury unless the archive route is already available.

Resource efficiency

Ration Rack and Drone Hook improve repeated actions rather than one-time milestones. They are strongest when purchased before many remaining scavenges and combats. Buying them after most locations are cleared provides less total value, though the Drone Hook can still help with late bosses.

Preparing for bosses

Bosses are recognizable because their rewards include relay keys or because they occupy the final encounter of a major location. Enter with full health, enough scrap for several repairs if the Field Repair Kit is installed, and enough damage that the fight will not last indefinitely.

The Relay Warden is an early test of whether damage and health upgrades have kept pace with exploration. The Buried Engine punishes entering the service tunnels with only starter equipment. The Echo Caretaker has a broad damage range and rewards guarding against dangerous turns. The Array Sentinel is the final equipment check before the Carrier.

Retreating is strategically correct when the remaining health cannot survive the enemy’s likely next hit. The travel cost is usually cheaper than losing a percentage of a large signal reserve through emergency reboot.

Building the Carrier Key

The Carrier Key requires the Phase Array, Pulse Rifle, 90 scrap, 500 signal, 6 intel, and all 3 relay keys. The relay keys are consumed as part of the cost. This is intentional: they are physical components used to authorize access rather than permanent badges.

When the Sky Array reads 1/2, the first encounter is complete but the Carrier remains locked. Build the Carrier Key at the Workbench, return to the Sky Array, and Explore again. A clear locked-state message now appears when the key is missing.

Before buying the key, maintain enough rations and power to travel back to the array. It is frustrating—but recoverable—to finish the expensive build and then need another scavenging cycle simply to leave the shelter.

Recovering from a stalled run

If signal is low, leave the game open briefly after strengthening the antenna path. If scrap is low, scavenge at Node 7 or fight remaining ordinary encounters. If rations are low, prioritize scavenging and the Ration Rack. If intel is low after the archive is complete, use repeatable decoding. If combat is too difficult, retreat and finish lower-range locations before returning.

No ordinary resource state requires a reset. The campaign is designed so the player can rebuild from defeat, and updates preserve existing browser saves. A reset is best reserved for replaying the story with a different purchase order rather than repairing a mistake.

Endgame choice

The final choice has no mechanical “best” outcome. Open Channel values plurality, uncertainty, and the return of many independent voices. Perfect Signal values stability, coordination, and the end of error. The game’s systems support both interpretations: the network is dangerous because it eliminates difference, but it is also the only infrastructure capable of ending the storm.

Players interested in both conclusions should export a save after defeating the Carrier but before choosing at the Far Horizon. That preserves a branching point without replaying the entire campaign.
